# Progression Philosophy

## Overview

Echoes of the Wilds is built around player-driven progression.

Rather than following a predetermined storyline, players create their own journey through the choices they make. The game provides opportunities, activities, challenges, and discoveries, but it does not force players onto a single path.

Every player's story should feel unique.

---

## Core Principle

There is no required story.

Players decide who they become through their actions.

One player may spend their early adventures collecting shells along the coast, while another may focus on exploring, crafting, taming creatures, hunting monsters, or pursuing countless other activities.

Over time, players may discover new interests and evolve their playstyle naturally.

A collector may become a hunter.

A hunter may become a merchant.

A merchant may become an explorer.

The player's choices create their story.

---

## Character Levels

Characters gain levels as they grow and experience the world.

Levels represent a player's overall progression and development.

Experience can be earned through many different activities, not just combat.

Possible sources of experience include:

* Exploration
* Combat
* Crafting
* Gathering
* Taming
* Discoveries
* Quests
* Community activities
* Other forms of gameplay

Players should never feel forced into a single activity to progress.

---

## Skill Progression

Skills improve through use.

The more a player participates in an activity, the more skilled they become in that area.

Skills reflect experience gained through action rather than simple level requirements.

Players grow naturally through the activities they choose to pursue.

---

## Flexible Character Development

Echoes of the Wilds does not use rigid class restrictions.

Players are free to develop their character however they choose.

Characters can learn multiple skills and pursue many different playstyles over time.

Players are encouraged to create unique builds that reflect their own goals and adventures.

---

## Freedom of Choice

There is no "correct" way to play.

Players are encouraged to set their own goals and pursue their own interests.

Whether someone wants to become a collector, explorer, hunter, crafter, merchant, tamer, or a combination of many roles, the game should support their journey.

---

## Long-Term Growth

A single character can eventually learn every skill in the game.

Mastery should require significant time, effort, and dedication.

Players should always have opportunities to continue growing and expanding their capabilities.

---

## Design Goal

The purpose of progression is not to tell the player's story.

The purpose of progression is to give players the freedom to create their own stories through meaningful choices, personal goals, and unique adventures.

Every player's journey should feel different.

Every journey leaves an echo.
